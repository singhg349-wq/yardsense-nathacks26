# Story script: "Monday 7:10 AM" (3 min)

> For the 5-minute presentation, use `script-5min.md`. This file is the 3-minute opening story.

Speaker: pitch owner. Pace: about 130 words a minute. Illustrative numbers only; the group is the fictional Northgate Group.

---

[slide: black. Clock: 7:10 AM, Monday]

It's Monday, 7:10 in the morning.

[slide: Priya at Riverside Clinic, request card: 2 scissor lifts, 1 telehandler, 1 45 kW generator, 10 weeks]

Priya is the superintendent on Riverside Clinic. She needs two scissor lifts, a telehandler and a generator for ten weeks. She sends the request to Dana.

[slide: Dana, three tabs: SAP, spreadsheet, phone]

Dana is the equipment coordinator at Northgate Builders. Her tools are an SAP asset register, a spreadsheet that's already out of date, and a phone. SAP knows what the group owns. It doesn't know where anything is, or whether it's free.

Forty-five minutes later, she books everything from an outside rental company. That's not a mistake. That's what you do at 7:55 on a Monday with a site waiting.

[slide: the same morning, three hidden facts appear one by one]

Here's what Dana couldn't see.

The group's own telehandler has been sitting idle at Westgate Tower since framing finished.

Northgate Rentals, our own sister company, has two lifts parked in its yard.

And four upcoming projects in Procore each plan to rent a generator. Four separate rentals, and nobody has added them up. When we do add them up, buying still loses money, and the engine says so.

[slide: $23,208 leaving the "Northgate Group" box]

Twenty-three thousand dollars left the group for one request, while three of our own machines sat idle. And nobody did anything wrong.

Here's why. To one project, a $900 week from our rental company and a $950 week from outside look the same. To the group, one is money staying in the family on a unit that was idle anyway. The other is $950 gone. The cheapest answer for a project isn't the cheapest answer for the group, and nobody's job is to look at the group.

[slide: YardSense logo. "Move ours. Rent from sister. Rent outside. Buy."]

That's YardSense. It reads SAP and Procore exports the group already has, plus a check-out log kept in the app. No trackers. It looks at every project's future demand and tells Dana, for every request: move ours, rent from our sister company, rent outside, or have the sister company buy it.

[slide: cut to the live app]

So let's go back to 7:10 on Monday. Here's Priya's request. Gurshmeer, take it from here.

[handoff: Gurshmeer starts the demo with the CSV upload]
