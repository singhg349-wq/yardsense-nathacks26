Problem: In a construction group, equipment decisions are made project by project, by phone, under time pressure. The SAP asset register knows what the group owns but not what is free; schedules live in Procore; the check-out log is a spreadsheet. Rental dollars leave the group while its own equipment sits idle, and nobody adds up demand across projects to see when buying is cheaper.

Solution: YardSense ranks four options for every request — move ours, rent from sister, rent outside, buy — by group cost, with project cost shown alongside. It reads SAP, Procore and the check-out log; no trackers. Availability combines the log with schedule inference. The cost model includes transport, wear and buy break-even across the pipeline. The UI explains every number and labels every data source. The live plan uses a greedy allocator; an exact PuLP/CBC static comparison puts min-cost 2.2% below greedy ($2,825 on 16 requests).

Why different: Tenna tracks past usage with hardware. Procore Equipment looks at current project usage, one company at a time. HCSS and the rental ERPs run dispatch and billing. YardSense is the group-level decision layer on future demand, with no hardware.

Demo: Dana, equipment coordinator at the fictional Northgate Group, handles Riverside Clinic's request for two scissor lifts, a telehandler and a 45 kW generator. The scissor lifts come from the sister rental company, the telehandler moves from an idle site, and the generator is bought because four pipeline projects need one and it breaks even within six months. Dragging Kingsway six weeks later (a what-if) flips the generator to rent outside. The same change can be triggered by importing a revised Procore CSV.

Impact: A deterministic 12-month synthetic replay of 96 requests shows $137,859 of $275,391 outside spend avoided (50.1%), with 35 rental leaks. The fleet advisor recommends adding two boom lifts and two heaters and retiring a mini excavator and a plate compactor. Public analogs: Caltrans found 9 of 32 owned units available while renting; NC DOT found 67% of heavy equipment used less than half the time.

What is real: FastAPI API, SQLite, availability, cost model, transport matrix, greedy allocator, PuLP/CBC static min-cost comparison, backtest, fleet advisor, six React screens, CSV upload, provenance, 59 backend and 89 frontend tests. Mocked or roadmap: live SAP/Procore/Textura APIs, real history, the Textura leak report, a dynamic optimizer, auth and mobile. Most rates are labelled placeholders; two 4-week rates are sourced. The team is five CS students; industry guidance came from a construction professional. Next step: a 60-day read-only pilot on one group's real export.
