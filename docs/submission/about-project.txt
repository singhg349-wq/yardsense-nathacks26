Problem: In a construction group, equipment decisions are made project by project, by phone, under time pressure. The SAP asset register knows what the group owns but not what is free; schedules live in Procore; the check-out log is a spreadsheet. Rental dollars leave the group while its own equipment sits idle, and nobody adds up demand across projects to see when buying is cheaper.

Solution: YardSense ranks four options for every request — move ours, rent from sister, rent outside, buy — by group cost, with project cost shown alongside. It reads SAP and Procore CSV exports and a check-out log kept in the app; no trackers. Availability combines the log with schedule inference. The cost model includes transport, wear and a buy test on each extra unit's 12-month net. The UI explains every number and labels every data source. The live plan uses a greedy allocator; on a static snapshot of 16 requests, an exact PuLP/CBC solver is 2.1% ($2,825) below a greedy pass under the same model.

Why different: Tenna is tracker-led. Procore Equipment plans equipment by project. HCSS and the rental ERPs run dispatch and billing. In our public review, none shows ranking all four options across a group from future schedules.

Demo: Dana, equipment coordinator at the fictional Northgate Group, handles Riverside Clinic's request for two scissor lifts, a telehandler and a 45 kW generator. The scissor lifts come from the sister rental company, the telehandler moves from an idle site, and the generator is rented outside: one more generator would save $10,050 of rent against $13,800 a year to own, so the engine refuses to buy. Dragging Kingsway six weeks later (a what-if) ripples across projects: Kingsway takes the two idle sister-yard lifts, so Riverside's and Capilano's scissor lifts go to outside rental. The same change can be triggered by importing a revised Procore CSV.

Impact: A deterministic 12-month synthetic replay of 96 requests shows $48,112 of $286,266 outside spend avoided (16.8%), with 15 rental leaks. Its availability rates are our assumed inputs, so it shows the method, not a forecast. The fleet advisor recommends retiring one idle mini excavator and adding nothing. Public analogs: a 2018 Caltrans audit found owned units available for 9 of 32 rentals justified by breakdowns; NC auditors found two-thirds of sampled DOT heavy equipment used under half the time.

What is real: FastAPI API, SQLite, availability, cost model, transport matrix, greedy allocator, PuLP/CBC static min-cost comparison, backtest, fleet advisor, six React screens, CSV upload, provenance, 106 backend and 105 frontend tests. Mocked or roadmap: live SAP/Procore/Textura APIs, real history, the Textura leak report, a dynamic optimizer, auth and mobile. Most rates are labelled placeholders; two 4-week rates are sourced. The team is five CS students; industry guidance came from a construction professional. Next step: a 60-day read-only pilot on one group's real export.
