YardSense is a group-level equipment decision engine for construction groups that own a rental company. When a project requests a machine, it ranks four options by what the whole group pays: move an idle group-owned unit, rent from the sister rental company, rent outside, or have the sister company buy it. It shows project cost beside group cost and explains every recommendation. It needs no trackers: it reads SAP asset-register and Procore schedule exports, plus a check-out log kept in the app. Unlike fleet-tracking tools, it looks at future demand across the whole group, not one company's past usage. The demo follows Dana, an equipment coordinator, through a Riverside Clinic request. The engine refuses a buy that doesn't pay (a generator would lose $3,750 a year, so it rents outside), and one slipped schedule ripples across projects. A synthetic 12-month replay shows $48,112 of $286,266 outside spend avoided (16.8%), with 15 rental leaks; its availability rates are our assumed inputs, so it shows the method, not a forecast. On a static snapshot of 16 requests, an exact PuLP solver is 2.1% below a greedy pass under the same model; the live plan stays greedy and explainable. The data is synthetic and labelled; the engine, API, screens and 205 tests are real.
